Tilda Swinton — Ongoing is a collaborative exhibition at Onassis Ready in Agios Ioannis Rentis, gathering works by eight of Swinton's closest artistic collaborators. You step into 3,760 square metres of converted plastics factory and a rack of clothes hangs without vitrine, close enough to read the wear.

Pedro Almodovar, Luca Guadagnino, Joanna Hogg, Derek Jarman, Jim Jarmusch, Olivier Saillard, Tim Walker, and Apichatpong Weerasethakul each contribute work made from direct creative proximity to Swinton. The exhibition originated at Amsterdam's Eye Filmmuseum and travels to Athens in co-production with Onassis Culture. Highlights include Jarmusch's Zelda Winston — a re-edited installation drawn from The Dead Don't Die — Hogg's Flat 19, a multimedia reconstruction of Swinton's 1980s London apartment, and Almodovar's The Human Voice in its first exhibition presentation. Saillard's A Biographical Wardrobe — a live performance where Swinton animates pieces from her personal clothing collection — runs on select opening dates with separate tickets.

If you want wall text and a clean museum path, this former factory operates on looser terms. But if you want to see how eight directors and artists see the same person across forty years of shared work, this is the show.

Onassis Ready is at Stratis Tsirka 2 — Eleonas metro is the nearest station, roughly a twenty-five-minute walk or a short taxi. Bus 838 stops about 300 metres from the venue. Tickets are €15 through the Onassis website. The run is May 16 through June 28.

When it closes, the clothes go back in the closet.
