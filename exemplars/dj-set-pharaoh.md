A needle drops on vinyl and the first notes of something between afrojazz and Brazilian tropicalia fill the room from a stereo system built to make records sound the way the engineers intended. You are sitting where the wood-fire grill sends a faint smoke note across your glass of natural wine, and the person behind the turntable is selecting the next record from a floor-to-ceiling metal cabinet that holds the entire collection in plain sight.

Pharaoh is a wine bar, restaurant, and listening room on Solomou 54 in Exarchia, opened in late 2022 by travel journalist Fotis Vallatos, chef Manolis Papoutsakis, baritone Dimitris Platanias, and wine specialist Perry Panagiotakopoulos. The concept fuses a Greek kafeneio, a French brasserie, and a Japanese jazz kissa — a listening bar where the music is curated with the same attention as the wine list. The kitchen operates without electricity or gas, cooking exclusively over wood-fired stoves and charcoal grills. The wine selection runs to over 400 natural labels. The Michelin Bib Gourmand arrived quickly. The Vinyl Selection nights put a rotating cast of the city's DJs behind the mezzanine turntable, playing only from records.

The crowd on a vinyl night is the intersection of Athens food culture and record culture. Couples splitting a bottle and half-listening to the music as atmosphere. Solo visitors at the bar who came specifically because they follow the DJ rotation. The room transitions as the evening progresses: early arrivals eat; later arrivals drink and listen; by late evening the energy shifts from dining to something closer to a low-key club night.

The evening has no set list and no fixed arc. The DJ reads the room and the records dictate the journey — a Brazilian samba cut might give way to a Turkish psych track, then settle into a long jazz side. As dinner plates clear, the volume and the tempo adjust. By midnight, the mezzanine becomes the center of gravity and the room finds a rhythm that dinner service only hinted at.

If you want a high-energy club night with a dance floor and a DJ booth built for spectacle, Pharaoh operates at a volume where conversation remains possible and the music rewards attention rather than demanding movement. But if you want an evening where the wine, the food, and the records are all chosen with the same care, and the room lets you move between eating, drinking, and listening at your own pace, this is the format.

Pharaoh is at Solomou 54 in Exarchia. The nearest metro is Omonia on the Red line, roughly a ten-minute walk. Panepistimio on the Blue line is a similar distance. No cover charge — the Vinyl Selection runs from 20:30 onward. Reservations through the Pharaoh website or by calling +30 210 380 8412 are recommended for dinner; bar seating is walk-in.

A kitchen that cooks with fire, a wine list of 400 natural labels, and a DJ playing only from vinyl — the combination exists in one room in Exarchia, and the Vinyl Selection nights are when all three elements operate at full attention.
