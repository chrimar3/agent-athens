Tartuffe by Moliere plays at Theatro Alkyonis near Victoria Square, directed by Elena Mavridou with Giannis Tsortekis in the title role. You step into a monument-classified 1960s cinema and a con artist banned by Louis XIV in 1664 steps onstage to work his grift again.

Tsortekis carries the particular danger a good Tartuffe demands — the charm has to be real enough that you understand why Orgon believes it. The pleasure is architectural: Moliere builds the trap in plain sight, and you watch the family walk into it one member at a time while the maid says exactly what you are thinking.

If you want contemplative silence and polite applause, this is not that night. If you want seventeenth-century satire that lands like last week's news, Moliere still has the sharpest pen in the room.

Theatro Alkyonis is at Ioulianou 42-46, two minutes from Victoria metro. Curtain at 19:00, open seating — arrive early. Tickets €20 through more.com.

Moliere has been dead for 353 years and still has not run out of hypocrites to write about.
