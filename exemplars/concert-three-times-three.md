The room is small enough that you can hear the dancers' feet on the floor before the music starts. Three musicians sit on one side. Three dancers stand on the other. Nobody knows who will work with whom until the moment arrives — the pairings are drawn at random, and each duo gets fifteen minutes to build something from nothing. You are watching the negotiation happen in real time.

Three Times Three is a monthly improvisation series curated by composer Spyros Polychronopoulos (known as Spyweirdos) at the Kentro Elenchou Tileoraseon — the Television Control Center — in Athens. The format is precise: three musicians meet three dancers, random pairs improvise for fifteen minutes each, and then all six performers join for a thirty-minute collective improvisation to close the evening. Edition number five brings together drummer Yiannis Notaras, saxophonist Dimitris Pantelias, and vocalist Anastasia Kotsali alongside dancers Maria Kasidokosta, Dorothea Polychronopoulou, and Anastasia Valsamaki. The performers come from contemporary dance and experimental music backgrounds, and the improvisation operates at a professional level — this is not a jam session.

The crowd for a series like this draws from two communities that rarely overlap elsewhere: the contemporary dance circuit and the experimental music scene. You will find choreographers watching how musicians respond to physical cues, and sound artists studying how dancers translate rhythm into gesture. The audience is small by design — this is not programming that aims to fill a stadium, and the intimacy is the point.

The evening builds through accumulation. The first duo finds its language — maybe the drummer follows the dancer's breath, maybe the dancer resists the rhythm entirely. The second pairing discovers a different dynamic. By the third, the audience has learned to read the improvisation alongside the performers. The closing collective piece draws on everything that came before, and the six performers must navigate each other's vocabularies simultaneously. Some editions lock into a shared rhythm so tightly the performers look rehearsed; others reveal productive friction. Neither outcome is a failure.

If you want a polished performance with a known setlist and rehearsed transitions, improvisation series operate on different terms — the risk of the unknown is the offer. But if you are drawn to the moment where a saxophonist and a dancer find a shared pulse without speaking a word, and you want to witness that discovery happening fifteen minutes at a time, Three Times Three makes the process visible.

The Television Control Center is at Kyprou 91A and Sikinou 35, in the Ampelokipoi area. Start time is 21:00. Tickets are €10 — available through more.com or by calling 213 00 40 496. The venue is not directly on a metro line, but Ampelokipoi metro on the Blue line is a fifteen-minute walk, and taxis from the center run €8-10. The monthly cadence means missing one edition is not permanent — but each edition's random pairings produce combinations that will not recur.

No two editions of Three Times Three produce the same performance, because the format guarantees that the performers cannot prepare for each other. This is the fifth iteration, and the series has earned its audience through a structure that makes repetition impossible.
