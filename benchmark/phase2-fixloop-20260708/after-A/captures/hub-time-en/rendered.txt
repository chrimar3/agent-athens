In Detail
Barbara Kruger: Untitled (Pride and Contempt)

ΚΠΙΣΝ · Tuesday 28 April 2026 · Free

A 90-meter text mural wraps the façade of the National Library of Greece, flanked by installations running both banks of the SNFCC Canal. Barbara Kruger has translated her vocabulary of power, belief, and public language into Greek for the first time — thirteen site-specific works commissioned by the Stavros Niarchos Foundation for the cultural center's outdoor spaces. This is Kruger's first solo exhibition in Greece, curated by Katerina Stathopoulou.

In Orbit | Οφ δε ρέκορντ

ΚΠΙΣΝ · Friday 10 July 2026 · Free

Light installations wrap the curved shell of the Dome in Stavros Niarchos Park, and for three July nights that space belongs to In Orbit — the festival the SNFCC (ΚΠΙΣΝ) hands to a different Athens record label each evening. On Friday 10 July, the label is Dum Dum Records, and the night opens the 2026 edition. Now in its seventh consecutive year, In Orbit reads the city's alternative scene rather than chasing it, letting each curator shape one night on its own terms.

Helloween

Πλατεία Νερού · Friday 10 July 2026 · Ticket

Three years after their 2023 set on the same Faliro stage, Helloween return to Plateia Nerou on Friday 10 July for Release Athens 2026. The Hamburg power-metal band headlines a bill that also runs Saxon and Ashes of Ares, with the open-air gates opening early at 18:00 on the coast.

Release Athens 2026 / Sabaton / Savatage / Epica

Πλατεία Νερού · Friday 10 July 2026 · Ticket

The first notes arrive as a wall—not guitar, but an architecture of sound that suggests medieval fortifications and modern studio production existing in the same moment. Sabaton takes the waterfront stage, flanked by Savatage and Epica, and suddenly the platform becomes a space where European heavy music reveals its full sophistication.

Release Athens 2026 / Helloween, Saxon & more

Πλατεία Νερού · Friday 10 July 2026 · Ticket

The opening riff carries the weight of German metal history—not the brutality of Teutonic origins, but the precision and musicianship that German bands brought to heavy music in the 1980s. Helloween takes the waterfront stage, followed by Saxon, and suddenly you're in the presence of bands that wrote the template for what European metal could become when it decided to prioritize technical mastery as fiercely as aggression. Helloween emerged from Hamburg in 1984 and essentially defined power metal: a fusion of New Wave of British Heavy Metal (NWOBHM) precision with German technical sophistication and production values.

Ταφ Λάθος

Τεχνόπολη · Friday 10 July 2026 · €10

Taf Lathos has spent 20 years in Greek hip-hop — six albums and a long list of collaborations — and marks the milestone with the biggest show of his career, 'I Lampsi Edo De Svinei' (The Shine Here Does Not Fade), with his live band at Technopolis. Two decades in, this reads as a career-summing night rather than a tour stop — a rapper who built his following on resistance and craft, now on a major Athens stage. Technopolis stages its summer concerts open-air in the yard of the former Gazi gasworks, under the old industrial chimneys; arrive early to cross the complex to the stage. Doors at 20:00, a short walk from Kerameikos metro.

Queen G

Island Athens Riviera · Friday 10 July 2026 · €10

The Vouliagmeni sea sits just below the deck as Queen G plays Island on Friday 10 July at 20:00, folding afro-house grooves and soulful electronic textures into a Riviera summer night. Athinorama bills the set around rolling percussion and atmospheric rhythms — an open-air party rather than a seated concert.

The Storyville Ragtimers: Α tribute to Louis Armstrong «What a Wonderful World»

Μέγαρο Μουσικής Αθηνών · Friday 10 July 2026 · €25

The Storyville Ragtimers founded in Athens in 2015, named for the New Orleans red-light district where jazz first took commercial form between Buddy Bolden and the 1917 closure that scattered the players up the Mississippi. On Friday 10 July 2026 at 20:30, the ensemble takes the Megaron Garden with a tribute to Louis Armstrong — tracing his arc from the Hot Five recordings of the 1920s through to the 1967 chart standard that gave the show its title. The ensemble's influences run through King Oliver — Armstrong's mentor in the Chicago years — alongside Jelly Roll Morton, Bix Beiderbecke, Eddie Lang, Sidney Bechet, Fats Waller, and the Original Dixieland Jazz Band, the New Orleans group that cut the first commercial jazz record in 1917.

What to Expect

Weekends in Athens right now are packed. The indoor season is at full power and the first open-air events are appearing — which means overlap. You can catch a gallery opening in Kolonaki on Friday afternoon, a live concert in Gazi on Friday night, an exhibition and a matinee theater show on Saturday, and a rebetiko afternoon on Sunday that slides into evening without anyone noticing.

Friday and Saturday nights are the main event. Gazi clubs fill after midnight. Exarchia’s live music venues start at 21:00–22:00 and run late. The theater scene peaks on weekend evenings across 100+ stages. If you’re choosing between options — and in Athens right now, you will be — use the curated picks at the top of this page.

Sunday is an underrated cultural day in Athens. Matinee rebetiko is a genuine experience (Stoa Athanaton in the Psyri area, starting at 13:00). The Acropolis Museum stays open late in the evenings. SNFCC often runs its biggest family programming on Sundays. And the flea market at Monastiraki Square is an event in itself — arrive before 11:00 for the best finds.

For this weekend specifically: check whether any national holidays fall on the calendar. Greece has frequent public holidays that can extend weekends and change venue schedules. Museum open entry days (first Sunday of the month, November–March) are worth planning around. The weather in March is usually mild enough for walking between neighborhoods but carry a layer — Athens evenings still cool down after sunset.
