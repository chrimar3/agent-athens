Salt air drifts through the doors of the Asteria Domus in Glyfada each time someone steps onto the terrace overlooking the Saronic Gulf. Inside, the conversation is about GPU clusters, inference costs, and deployment architectures at scale. The InfraAI Global Summit runs March 30 through April 1 along the Athenian Riviera, and it gathers just over 200 attendees -- a deliberately small number for a deliberately specific audience.

You walk into a room where C-suite executives and investors sit alongside the engineers who actually provision the compute. The focus here is AI infrastructure: not what models can do in a demo, but what it takes to run them reliably, affordably, and at production scale. The conversations cut to procurement cycles, data center strategy, chip supply constraints, and the cost curves that determine whether an AI product survives its first year.

The venue choice signals intent. Asteria Domus sits on the coastal stretch south of Athens center, in the affluent suburb of Glyfada. The setting is built for the kind of event where the hallway conversations carry as much weight as the scheduled sessions. With 200-plus attendees rather than 2,000, you encounter the same people at coffee breaks, at lunch, on the seaside terrace at dusk. Relationships form through repetition and proximity over three days, not through exchanged business cards at a crowded expo hall.

The ticket price sits at approximately one thousand euros, which functions as its own filter. The people in the room have budget authority or direct influence over infrastructure decisions. This is not a place to learn the basics of cloud computing; it is a place where procurement meets architecture meets capital allocation.

For those operating in the AI infrastructure layer -- building platforms, selling compute, investing in the picks-and-shovels of the current wave -- the summit compresses months of relationship-building into a long weekend on the Mediterranean coast. The intimacy of the gathering means speakers are accessible, panels stay focused, and questions from the floor come from people with operational context.

If you make decisions about AI compute spend, manage deployment pipelines at scale, or allocate capital to infrastructure plays, this room is built for you. If you are exploring AI as a general interest or looking for introductory content, the price and focus will feel misaligned.

Late March on the Athenian Riviera brings mild sun, clear light, and water cold enough to discourage swimming but warm enough to make the terrace comfortable. The combination of focused technical discourse and coastal geography creates a setting where strategic conversations unfold at a pace the content deserves.
