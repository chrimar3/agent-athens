O Karagiozis sto Paraga is a shadow puppet theater performance at Paraga in Neos Kosmos, Athens, on 19 April 2026. The screen glows white, a hand-cut figure lurches into frame, and thirty children inhale.

Tasos Konstas has spent thirty years behind the shadow screen — Evgenios Spatharis named him successor before his death in 2009. This Sunday Konstas adapts Aristophanes Peace: Karagiozis rides a beetle to Olympus and bargains with the gods to free Eirini from captivity. Ancient comedy and shadow puppetry run on the same fuel — a commoner outwitting the powerful through stubbornness and bad jokes.

If your children need screens and surround sound, a backlit cotton sheet will test them. If they can laugh at a flat figure with an oversized arm for fifty minutes, the room does the rest.

Paraga is at Kallirois 89, two blocks from Syngrou-Fix metro. Curtain at 11:30, tickets eight to ten euros via more.com. A figure-making workshop follows for ages five and up.

Antiwar comedy older than Christianity, performed by a character thrown out of windows since the Ottoman occupation.
